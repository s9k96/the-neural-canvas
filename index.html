A world made only of straight lines would never produce circles, spirals, branching trees, or coastlines. Nature isn't linear—it emerges from simple rules interacting in nonlinear ways.

A neural network without activation functions sees the world through a single straight boundary, no matter how many layers it has. Add a ReLU, and that changes. One simple nonlinear rule gives the network the ability to bend, split, and shape its decision boundary to match the complexity of the data.

The activation function isn't just another layer—it's the moment the model stops thinking in straight lines.
